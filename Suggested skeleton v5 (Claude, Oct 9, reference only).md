## 1. Introduction

**¶1 · context · ~65 words.** *Topic claim:* IBD is a lifelong, relapsing disease whose burden is rising fastest where diets are Westernising. [v3]
- **Two diseases:** CD is patchy and transmural, mostly in the terminal ileum and proximal colon, and causes strictures and fistulae. UC is continuous and mucosal, running from the rectum into the colon. [Dolinger 2024; Le Berre 2023]
- **Symptoms and consequences:** diarrhoea (bloody in UC), abdominal pain, fatigue and weight loss. Longer term: bowel damage, colectomy in 10–20% of people with UC, colorectal cancer risk and poorer quality of life. [Le Berre 2023]
- **Why diet** [v3]: incidence is rising in newly industrialised regions that are adopting Western diets, which implicates diet as an environmental driver. [Le Berre 2023; Ratajczak 2023]

**¶2 · question · ~35 words.** *Topic claim:* whether MD counts as an alternative depends on which part of management it serves.
- **Define and claim:** *management* means inducing remission, then maintaining it, for life. Thesis: drugs can break the disrupted loop, and MD may keep the healthy loop running. That makes MD a maintenance alternative alongside drugs, not a replacement.
- **Roadmap:** §2 compares the healthy and disrupted loops, §3 matches each disruption with an MD repair, §4 tests this in patients, and §5 sets the limits.

> **Link (cause):** the case for diet rests on how diet shapes the gut loop, in health and in IBD.

## 2. How the healthy gut loop breaks in IBD, and where drugs act

**¶1 · the loop and its two inputs · ~45 words.** *Topic claim:* a healthy colon runs a self-reinforcing loop of three nodes, fed by two dietary inputs. [v3]
- **The loop:** ① microbes ferment fibre to short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), mainly butyrate; ② butyrate sets how colonocytes make energy, which sets the oxygen gradient and the barrier; ③ microbial and lipid signals set immune tone, which feeds back on ②. The two inputs are **fibre** and **fat quality**. [Litvak 2018; Parada Venegas 2019; Smyth 2024]

**¶2 · three paired contrasts · ~175 words.** *Topic claim:* a Western diet attacks the loop from both sides at once, and inflammation then holds it open. [v3, corrected]
- **① Fermentation and the oxygen gradient (R1, R2).** *Healthy:* fibre feeds obligate anaerobes that make butyrate, and the anaerobic lumen keeps those anaerobes dominant. *Disrupted:* low fibre leaves fewer butyrate producers, while oxygen and nitrate escaping the epithelium let facultative anaerobes such as *Escherichia coli* expand. [Parada Venegas 2019; Litvak 2018]
- **② Epithelial energy and membrane lipids (R3, R5).** *Healthy:* butyrate activates PPARγ, so colonocytes burn fatty acids and hold their surface below 1% oxygen; membrane fatty acids stay balanced. *Disrupted:* without butyrate, PPARγ falls silent, colonocytes switch to glycolysis and the hypoxia is lost; on the fat side, linoleic acid, the main n-6 fat of seed oils, becomes arachidonic acid in colonocyte membranes. [Litvak 2018; Tjønneland 2009; Smyth 2024]
  - Human data, one axis each: UC colonocytes oxidise less butyrate and show lower PPARγ [Litvak 2018], and the highest linoleic acid intake gave an odds ratio of 2.49 for UC in a European cohort, although other cohorts did not reproduce it. [Tjønneland 2009; Ananthakrishnan 2014†]
  - Where XBP1 or GPX4 defences are impaired, excess n-6 **or** n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) triggers endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and chemokine release, and PUFA intake tracked CD activity. [Schwärzler 2022]
- **③ Immune mediators, and the feedback that holds the loop open (R6, R7, R8).** *Healthy:* butyrate promotes *Foxp3*⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs) and IL-10, and the n-3 fats EPA and DHA yield pro-resolving mediators. *Disrupted:* Tregs fall, NF-κB drives tumour necrosis factor (TNF), and arachidonic acid yields prostaglandins and leukotrienes that are raised in UC mucosa. [Parada Venegas 2019; Smyth 2024]
  - Inflammatory signals then push colonocytes further towards glycolysis, so the loop may sustain itself. Note the direction of that arrow: low butyrate is plausibly a **consequence** of inflammation as well as a cause, which is the first reason to doubt that diet alone can break an active loop. [Litvak 2018]

**¶3 · gap · ~60 words.** *Topic claim:* current drugs act mainly on node ③ and can break the disrupted loop, but none corrects either dietary input. [v3]
- **Drugs by goal** [v3, broadened]: [Le Berre 2023; Dolinger 2024]
  - Corticosteroids induce remission only; thiopurines maintain it.
  - Advanced therapies do both: anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23 or anti-IL-23 antibodies, JAK inhibitors and S1P modulators.
  - 5-aminosalicylates (mild-to-moderate UC) also act partly at node ②, as PPARγ agonists. [Litvak 2018]
  - Surgery treats refractory disease; 10–20% of people with UC need colectomy.

> **Link (consequence):** drugs can break the disrupted loop but correct neither input. The question is whether a diet that reverses both inputs can restore the loop.

## 3. How Mediterranean diet components may restore the loop

**¶1 · mechanism (nodes ① and ②) · ~125 words.** *Topic claim:* MD reverses both dietary inputs, and each component answers a specific contrast from §2. [v3]
- **Fibre answers contrast ① and the fibre half of contrast ② (R1, R3, R4)** [v3]:
  - Fibre, legumes and whole grains bring back butyrate producers. In quiescent UC, after 12 weeks of MD, faecal SCFA and butyrate were higher and *Ruminococcus bromii* was more abundant. [Haskey 2023]
  - Restored butyrate should re-engage PPARγ, the same receptor 5-aminosalicylates target, and with it the hypoxia and barrier support downstream of it. [Litvak 2018; Parada Venegas 2019]
- **Fat quality answers contrast ② on the lipid side, and R2 (R5)** [v3, refined]:
  - Olive oil acts here by **substitution**: as the main cooking fat it takes the place of linoleic acid-rich seed oils, which lowers the n-6 supply that feeds membrane arachidonic acid. Its benefit is defined by what it displaces rather than by an action of its own. [Hashash 2024; Smyth 2024]
  - A whole-food pattern also avoids the PUFA excess that stresses susceptible epithelium. [Schwärzler 2022]

**¶2 · mechanism and boundary (node ③) · ~105 words.** *Topic claim:* once both inputs are corrected, node ③ may swing back from amplifying inflammation to resolving it, but only where the loop is still partly intact.
- **Fibre and fish answer contrast ③ (R6, R7)** [v3]:
  - A recovering SCFA supply should restore Treg and IL-10 restraint, and faecal secretory IgA did rise on MD. [Parada Venegas 2019; Haskey 2023]
  - Moderate fish supplies EPA and DHA, which yield less inflammatory eicosanoids and pro-resolving mediators, while the lower n-6 intake leaves less arachidonic acid to compete with them. [Smyth 2024]
  - Fish-oil capsules alone probably did not maintain CD remission, which argues that the fat benefit depends on the whole pattern rather than on one added nutrient. [Lev-Tzion 2014]
- **Boundary (R8):**
  - UC colonocytes oxidise less butyrate while inflamed. [Litvak 2018]
  - Fibre can inflame the gut when fermenting microbes are lost. [Armstrong 2023]
  - Susceptible epithelium reacts to n-3 as well as n-6 fat. [Schwärzler 2022]
  - So MD can hold a loop that drugs have calmed, rather than break an active one. Its fibre arm should matter most in colonic disease (UC).

> **Link (consequence):** this predicts a maintenance benefit, visible as stable inflammation and recovering node readouts. §4 tests that prediction.

## 4. Human evidence: holding remission, not inducing it

**¶1 · result (maintenance) · ~110 words.** *Topic claim:* in quiescent UC, MD held inflammation low and moved the fibre-side readouts in the predicted direction.
- **Trial** (randomised controlled trial (RCT), n = 28, 12 weeks): [Haskey 2023]
  - Faecal calprotectin (FC) directly measures intestinal inflammation; levels above 100 µg/g predict relapse.
  - FC stayed unchanged on MD (p = 0.375) but rose on the habitual diet (p = 0.049). At week 12, it exceeded 100 µg/g in 20% of the MD group versus 75% of the habitual-diet group.
  - The R1 readouts (faecal SCFA and butyrate) were higher, and the R6 readout (secretory IgA) rose. Symptom scores were similar. The MD group had dietitian coaching, and there was no endoscopy.
- **What the two axes are each supported by, and are not:** the fibre axis has the only measured response to MD itself, while the fat axis has the stronger evidence that its input causes disease and no intervention readout at all, because no MD trial has measured membrane fatty acids or eicosanoids. The two arms are therefore both evidenced, but at opposite ends of the same argument.

**¶2 · comparison (induction) · ~60 words.** *Topic claim:* the same diet has not been shown to break an active disrupted loop, so the evidence does not support replacing drugs.
- **Induction:**
  - In active CD, MD matched the specific carbohydrate diet (SCD) for clinical remission (43.5% versus 46.5%), but biomarker response was rare: 4 of 13 for FC, and 1 of 28 for C-reactive protein (CRP). [Lewis 2021]
  - A 2026 meta-analysis found no remission advantage over control diets (OR 0.98) and no mucosal-healing data. [Singh 2026]
- **Guidelines and verdict:**
  - The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) advises MD "for their overall health and general well-being". It states that no diet has consistently reduced flares in adults. This is expert opinion. [Hashash 2024]
  - **Verify** the wording of the 2025 ECCO consensus. [Svolos 2025†]
  - So MD is a maintenance alternative alongside drugs, not a replacement.

> **Link (restriction):** the benefit depends on a partly intact loop, a tolerant epithelium and continuous intake, and these conditions set the challenges.

## 5. Challenges: weak evidence, fibre and fat intolerance, lapsed adherence

*Write each challenge as a boundary on a claim. Name the claim affected, the constraint, the consequence, and what remains supported.*

**¶1 · limitation (evidence and biology) · ~80 words.** *Topic claim:* two limits narrow the restoration claim: thin evidence, and loops that MD cannot repair.
- **Evidence:**
  - The trials are small (28–194 patients), short (6–12 weeks) and unblinded. They define MD differently, patients stay on their drugs, and none reports mucosal healing. [Singh 2026]
  - Human readouts exist for R1 and R6 only. R4 and the whole fat arm rest on inference.
  - What remains supported: 12 weeks of FC maintenance in quiescent UC.
- **Loops MD cannot repair:**
  - In stricturing CD, transmural fibrosis narrows the lumen, so food needs to be softer and less fibrous. [Dolinger 2024; Hashash 2024]
  - Active IBD with depleted fermenting microbes reacts to intact β-fructan fibre through NLRP3 and TLR2. [Armstrong 2023]
  - Epithelium with impaired XBP1 or GPX4 is stressed even by n-3 fat. [Schwärzler 2022]
  - So the patients with the most disrupted loops tolerate the restoring inputs least.

**¶2 · limitation (real-world use) · ~80 words.** *Topic claim:* restoration lasts only while the inputs continue, so adherence is part of the mechanism.
- **Continuous inputs:**
  - Microbial and metabolite changes caused by exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) reverted when children returned to their habitual diet. So a loop restored by MD may drift back when the diet stops. [Gerasimidis 2014]
  - Real-world adherence is low, because patients avoid foods that provoke symptoms. [Ratajczak 2023]
  - Trial adherence, by contrast, relied on supplied meals or coaching. [Lewis 2021; Haskey 2023]
- **Access and culture** [v3]:
  - Dietitian access is not routine; the AGA only suggests it at diagnosis. [Hashash 2024]
  - The diet costs money, and it must be adapted to non-Mediterranean food cultures, as in a Chinese pilot RCT of a modified MD after CD surgery. [Huang 2026†]
  - App-based support is untested against inflammation. [v3]
- **Weighing and next test:**
  - Evidence gaps and adherence decide whether MD works at all; tolerance and access respond to dietetic support.
  - The decisive trial would last at least a year, use mucosal-healing endpoints, track readouts for **both** axes (faecal SCFA and secretory IgA, plus membrane fatty acids), and compare supported with unsupported adherence. Identifying the active components would follow. [v3]

> **Link (consequence):** weigh the restoration case against these limits to answer the title.

## 6. Conclusion

*About 60 words in 3–4 sentences, with nothing new. Write one sentence per slot:*

1. **Contribution:** IBD can be read as a healthy gut loop disrupted from two sides, too little fibre and too much n-6 fat. MD reverses both inputs and may restore the loop's fermentation, epithelial and immune nodes.
2. **Decisive evidence:** a small trial suggests that MD holds inflammation low in quiescent UC while the fibre-side readouts recover. No trial shows that it induces remission or mucosal healing, and none has measured the fat side.
3. **Implication:** MD therefore fits after drugs have broken the disrupted loop, as a maintenance strategy that keeps the healthy loop running.
4. **Boundary:** its benefit lasts only while the diet is eaten, and it awaits longer trials with mucosal-healing endpoints.
