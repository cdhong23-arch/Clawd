## 1. Introduction

**¶1 · context · ~65 words.** *Topic claim:* IBD is a lifelong, relapsing disease whose burden is rising fastest where diets are Westernising. [v3]
- **Two diseases:** CD is patchy and transmural, mostly in the terminal ileum and proximal colon, and causes strictures and fistulae. UC is continuous and mucosal, running from the rectum into the colon. [Dolinger 2024; Le Berre 2023]
- **Symptoms and consequences:** diarrhoea (bloody in UC), abdominal pain, fatigue and weight loss. Longer term: bowel damage, colectomy in 10–20% of people with UC, colorectal cancer risk and poorer quality of life. [Le Berre 2023]
- **Why diet** [v3]: incidence is rising in newly industrialised regions that are adopting Western diets, which implicates diet as an environmental driver. [Godny & Dotan 2024]

**¶2 · question · ~35 words.** *Topic claim:* whether MD counts as an alternative depends on which part of management it serves.
- **Define and claim:** *management* means inducing remission, then maintaining it, for life. Thesis: drugs can break the disrupted loop, and MD may keep the healthy loop running. That makes MD a maintenance alternative alongside drugs, not a replacement.
- **Roadmap:** §2 compares the healthy and disrupted loops, §3 matches each disruption with an MD repair, §4 tests this in patients, and §5 sets the limits.

> **Link (cause):** the case for diet rests on how diet shapes the gut loop, in health and in IBD.

## 2. How the healthy gut loop breaks in IBD, and where drugs act

**¶1 · mechanism (healthy loop) · ~80 words.** *Topic claim:* in health, two dietary inputs, fibre and a balanced fat supply, feed a self-reinforcing loop that keeps the gut quiet. [v3]
- **Healthy loop (Figure 1A; the "healthy" column of R1–R8):** [Litvak 2018; Parada Venegas 2019; Smyth 2024]
  - ① Fibre feeds obligate anaerobes that make short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), mainly butyrate. The anaerobic lumen keeps those anaerobes dominant.
  - ② Butyrate activates PPARγ, so colonocytes burn fatty acids and keep their surface hypoxic (below 1% oxygen). Hypoxia stabilises hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF), and tight junctions and mucus hold the barrier.
  - ③ Butyrate promotes *Foxp3*⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs) and IL-10. The n-3 fats EPA and DHA yield pro-resolving mediators that end inflammation.

**¶2 · mechanism (disrupted loop) · ~150 words.** *Topic claim:* a Western diet attacks the loop from two sides, too little fibre and too much n-6 fat. Inflammation then makes the damage self-sustaining. [v3, corrected]
- **① Microbes (R1, R2):**
  - Low fibre means fewer butyrate producers. [Parada Venegas 2019]
  - Oxygen and nitrate escaping the epithelium let facultative anaerobes such as *Escherichia coli* expand. [Litvak 2018]
- **② Epithelium (R3–R5):**
  - Fibre side: without butyrate, PPARγ falls silent, colonocytes switch to glycolysis, and hypoxia is lost. In UC, colonocytes oxidise less butyrate and the mucus is thinner. [Litvak 2018]
  - Fat side: linoleic acid, the main n-6 fat of seed oils, becomes arachidonic acid in colonocyte membranes. [Tjønneland 2009; Smyth 2024]
  - Where XBP1 or GPX4 defences are impaired, excess n-6 or n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) triggers endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and chemokine release. [Schwärzler 2022]
- **③ Immune tone and feedback (R6–R8):**
  - Tregs fall and NF-κB drives tumour necrosis factor (TNF).
  - Arachidonic acid yields pro-inflammatory prostaglandins and leukotrienes, which are raised in UC mucosa. [Parada Venegas 2019; Tjønneland 2009]
  - Inflammatory signals push colonocytes further towards glycolysis, so the loop may sustain itself. [Litvak 2018]
  - Human support for the fat side: the highest linoleic acid intake gave an odds ratio of 2.49 for UC in a European cohort [Tjønneland 2009], and PUFA intake tracked CD activity. [Schwärzler 2022]

*Budget note: if this paragraph runs long, keep one fibre step and one fat step per node. Move the rest into Figure 1B.*

**¶3 · gap · ~60 words.** *Topic claim:* current drugs act mainly on node ③ and can break the disrupted loop, but none corrects either dietary input. [v3]
- **Drugs by goal** [v3, broadened]: [Le Berre 2023; Dolinger 2024]
  - Corticosteroids induce remission only.
  - Thiopurines maintain remission.
  - Advanced therapies do both: anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23 or anti-IL-23 antibodies, JAK inhibitors and S1P modulators.
  - 5-aminosalicylates (mild-to-moderate UC) also act partly at node ②, as PPARγ agonists. [Litvak 2018]
  - Surgery treats refractory disease; 10–20% of people with UC need colectomy.

> **Link (consequence):** drugs can break the disrupted loop but correct neither input. The question is whether a diet that reverses both inputs can restore the loop.

## 3. How Mediterranean diet components may restore the loop

**¶1 · mechanism (nodes ① and ②) · ~130 words.** *Topic claim:* MD reverses both dietary inputs, and each component answers a specific §2 disruption. [v3]
- **Fibre answers R1, R3 and R4** [v3]:
  - Fibre, legumes and whole grains bring back butyrate producers. In quiescent UC, after 12 weeks of MD, faecal SCFA and butyrate were higher and *Ruminococcus bromii* was more abundant. [Haskey 2023]
  - Restored butyrate should re-engage PPARγ (the same receptor 5-aminosalicylates target) and, with it, hypoxia, HIF and tight junctions. [Litvak 2018; Parada Venegas 2019]
- **Fat quality answers R2 and R5** [v3, refined]:
  - Olive oil, rich in monounsaturated fat, replaces linoleic acid-rich seed oils. This lowers the n-6 load that feeds membrane arachidonic acid. [Hashash 2024; Smyth 2024]
  - A whole-food pattern also avoids the PUFA excess that stresses susceptible epithelium. [Schwärzler 2022]

**¶2 · mechanism and boundary (node ③) · ~110 words.** *Topic claim:* once both inputs are corrected, node ③ may swing back from amplifying inflammation to resolving it, but only where the loop is still partly intact.
- **Fibre and fish answer R6 and R7** [v3]:
  - A recovering SCFA supply should restore Treg and IL-10 restraint. [Parada Venegas 2019]
  - Moderate fish supplies EPA and DHA, which yield less inflammatory eicosanoids and pro-resolving mediators. [Smyth 2024]
  - Polyphenols add anti-inflammatory microbial metabolites. [Godny & Dotan 2024]
  - Faecal secretory IgA rose on MD. [Haskey 2023]
- **Boundary (R8):**
  - UC colonocytes oxidise less butyrate while inflamed. [Litvak 2018]
  - Fibre can inflame the gut when fermenting microbes are lost. [Armstrong 2023]
  - Susceptible epithelium reacts to n-3 as well as n-6 fat. [Schwärzler 2022]
  - So MD can hold a loop that drugs have calmed, rather than break an active one. Its fibre arm should matter most in colonic disease (UC).

> **Link (consequence):** this predicts a maintenance benefit, visible as stable inflammation and recovering node readouts. §4 tests that prediction.

## 4. Human evidence: holding remission, not inducing it

**¶1 · result (maintenance) · ~105 words.** *Topic claim:* in quiescent UC, MD held inflammation low and moved the node readouts in the predicted direction.
- **Trial** (randomised controlled trial (RCT), n = 28, 12 weeks): [Haskey 2023]
  - Faecal calprotectin (FC) directly measures intestinal inflammation; levels above 100 µg/g predict relapse.
  - FC stayed unchanged on MD (p = 0.375) but rose on the habitual diet (p = 0.049). At week 12, it exceeded 100 µg/g in 20% of the MD group versus 75% of the habitual-diet group.
  - The R1 readouts (faecal SCFA and butyrate) were higher, and the R6 readout (secretory IgA) rose. No trial has measured the fat-side readouts (R5, R7).
  - Symptom scores were similar. The MD group had dietitian coaching, and there was no endoscopy.

**¶2 · comparison (induction) · ~55 words.** *Topic claim:* the same diet has not been shown to break an active disrupted loop, so the evidence does not support replacing drugs.
- **Induction:**
  - In active CD, MD matched the specific carbohydrate diet (SCD) for clinical remission (43.5% versus 46.5%), but biomarker response was rare: 4 of 13 for FC, and 1 of 28 for C-reactive protein (CRP). [Lewis 2021]
  - A 2026 meta-analysis found no remission advantage over control diets (OR 0.98) and no mucosal-healing data. [Singh 2026]
- **Guidelines and verdict:**
  - The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) advises MD "for their overall health and general well-being". It states that no diet has consistently reduced flares in adults. This is expert opinion. [Hashash 2024]
  - **Verify** the wording of the 2025 ECCO consensus. [Svolos 2025†]
  - So MD is a maintenance alternative alongside drugs, not a replacement.
- **Figure 2 (optional, own drawing):** the evidence column of the comparison map, R1–R8, coloured by evidence level (human RCT, human association, mouse, inferred).

> **Link (restriction):** the benefit depends on a partly intact loop, a tolerant epithelium and continuous intake, and these conditions set the challenges.

## 5. Challenges: weak evidence, fibre and fat intolerance, lapsed adherence

*Write each challenge as a boundary on a claim. Name the claim affected, the constraint, the consequence, and what remains supported.*

**¶1 · limitation (evidence and biology) · ~75 words.** *Topic claim:* two limits narrow the restoration claim: thin evidence, and loops that MD cannot repair (the "no MD repair" row of the map).
- **Evidence:**
  - The trials are small (28–194 patients), short (6–12 weeks) and unblinded. They define MD differently, patients stay on their drugs, and none reports mucosal healing. [Singh 2026]
  - Human readouts exist for R1 and R6 only; the fat arm (R5, R7) has none.
  - Fish-oil capsules alone probably did not maintain CD remission. So any fat benefit likely depends on the whole pattern, not on adding one nutrient. [Lev-Tzion 2014†]
  - What remains supported: 12 weeks of FC maintenance in quiescent UC.
- **Loops MD cannot repair:**
  - In stricturing CD, transmural fibrosis narrows the lumen, so food needs to be softer and less fibrous. [Dolinger 2024; Hashash 2024]
  - Active IBD with depleted fermenting microbes reacts to intact β-fructan fibre through NLRP3 and TLR2. [Armstrong 2023]
  - Epithelium with impaired XBP1 or GPX4 is stressed even by n-3 fat. [Schwärzler 2022]
  - So the patients with the most disrupted loops tolerate the restoring inputs least.

**¶2 · limitation (real-world use) · ~75 words.** *Topic claim:* restoration lasts only while the inputs continue, so adherence is part of the mechanism.
- **Continuous inputs:**
  - Microbial and metabolite changes caused by exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) reverted when children returned to their habitual diet. So a loop restored by MD may drift back when the diet stops. [Gerasimidis 2014]
  - Real-world adherence is low, because patients avoid foods that provoke symptoms. [Ratajczak 2023]
  - Trial adherence, by contrast, relied on supplied meals or coaching. [Lewis 2021; Haskey 2023]
- **Access and culture** [v3]:
  - Dietitian access is not routine; the AGA only suggests it at diagnosis. [Hashash 2024]
  - The diet costs money.
  - MD must be adapted to non-Mediterranean food cultures, as in a Chinese pilot RCT of a modified MD after CD surgery. [Huang 2026†]
  - App-based support is untested against inflammation. [v3]
- **Weighing and next test:**
  - Evidence gaps and adherence decide whether MD works at all; tolerance and access respond to dietetic support.
  - The decisive trial would last at least a year and use mucosal-healing endpoints. It would track readouts for both arms (faecal SCFA, secretory IgA, membrane fatty acids) and compare supported with unsupported adherence.
  - Identifying the active components would follow. [v3]

> **Link (consequence):** weigh the restoration case against these limits to answer the title.

## 6. Conclusion

*About 60 words in 3–4 sentences, with nothing new. Write one sentence per slot:*

1. **Contribution:** IBD can be read as a healthy gut loop disrupted from two sides, too little fibre and too much n-6 fat. MD reverses both inputs and may restore the loop's fermentation, epithelial and immune nodes.
2. **Decisive evidence:** a small trial suggests that MD holds inflammation low in quiescent UC while the fibre-side readouts recover. No trial shows that it induces remission or mucosal healing.
3. **Implication:** MD therefore fits after drugs have broken the disrupted loop, as a maintenance strategy that keeps the healthy loop running.
4. **Boundary:** its benefit lasts only while the diet is eaten, and it awaits longer trials with mucosal-healing endpoints.
