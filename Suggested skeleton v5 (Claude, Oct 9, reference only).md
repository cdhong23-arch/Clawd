**Title (exact):** The Mediterranean diet as an alternative for the management of inflammatory bowel diseases

**Argument (rephrase it, or argue your own):** the Mediterranean diet (MD) is an alternative *maintenance* strategy used alongside drugs, not a replacement for them, and it is strongest in ulcerative colitis (UC). ## 1. Introduction
*~140 words · one paragraph*

- **Two diseases** [v3]: Crohn's disease (CD) — patchy, transmural, mainly terminal ileum/proximal colon → strictures, fistulae; UC — continuous, mucosal, rectum → colon; both chronic and relapsing. [Dolinger 2024; Le Berre 2023]
- **Burden:** diarrhoea (bloody in UC), pain, fatigue, weight loss; bowel damage; colectomy in 10–20% of UC; colorectal cancer risk; poorer quality of life. [Le Berre 2023]
- **Why diet** [v3]: incidence accelerating in newly industrialised, Westernising countries → partly dietary drivers. [Kaplan & Windsor 2021; Godny & Dotan 2024]
- **Define, then claim:** *management* = lifelong induction then maintenance; *alternative* = replacing drugs, replacing restrictive diets, or adding a long-term strategy → thesis: the evidence supports the third.

> **Hand-off →** With so many drugs available, why diet?

## 2. Diet-driven inflammation and the limits of drug therapy
*~200 words · ¶1 the loop · ¶2 drugs*

- **Trigger** [v3]: genes + environment → microbiota–barrier–immune imbalance. [Le Berre 2023]
- **Healthy loop (Figure 1):** fibre → microbial short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), mainly butyrate → colonocytes oxidise butyrate, using O₂ → hypoxia stabilises hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) → barrier; butyrate also inhibits histone deacetylases → *Foxp3*⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs). [Parada Venegas 2019]
- **Western-diet break** [v3, corrected]: low fibre → less butyrate → O₂ leak, dysbiosis, weaker barrier and Treg restraint [Parada Venegas 2019]; excess n-6 *or* n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) → epithelial ER stress → chemokines → enteritis where XBP1/GPX4 defences fail [Schwärzler 2022]; TNF-driven damage closes the loop.
- **Drugs by goal** [v3, broadened]: steroids induce only; 5-aminosalicylates in mild–moderate UC only; thiopurines maintain; advanced therapies (anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23 or IL-23, JAK inhibitors, S1P modulators) do both; surgery if refractory. [Le Berre 2023; Dolinger 2024]
- **The gap** [v3]: anti-TNF-naive CD — 23.8% primary non-response, 63.1% not in remission at week 54 [Kennedy 2019]; drugs act *downstream* on immunity, leaving *upstream* dietary and microbial causes untouched.
- **Figure 1 (own drawing, e.g. BioRender):** the loop in health vs Western diet; drug targets at the immune node (downstream), MD inputs at the microbial and barrier nodes (upstream); cite in §2 and §3.

> **Hand-off →** Can a whole diet repair the upstream causes, with evidence beyond mice?

## 3. How Mediterranean diet components may restore gut homeostasis
*~170 words · one or two paragraphs*

- **A pattern, not a formula** [v3]: vegetables, legumes, fruit, whole grains, nuts, olive oil, fish; few ultra-processed foods; everyday food acting in combination. [Hashash 2024; Godny & Dotan 2024]
- **Fibre → SCFA (Figure 1):** restores the HIF–barrier and Treg arms; in quiescent UC, stool SCFA and butyrate were higher after 12 weeks of MD than on the usual diet. [Haskey 2023; Parada Venegas 2019]
- **Fat quality** [v3, refined]: olive-oil monounsaturated fat replaces linoleic-acid-rich oils — soybean oil worsened colitis in susceptible mice, olive oil did not [Deol 2023†]; but n-3 also feeds the §2 ER-stress route, so the benefit of fish likely depends on dose and genotype. [Schwärzler 2022]
- **Polyphenols** [v3]: anti-inflammatory, partly via microbial metabolites; mostly preclinical. [Godny & Dotan 2024]
- **Limit:** fibre-free exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) induces remission in paediatric CD while *lowering* butyrate and *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii* [Gerasimidis 2014] → the fibre → SCFA logic may fit UC (colonic, mucosal) better than CD (ileal, transmural); present this as plausible, not proven.

> **Hand-off →** Plausible, more so for UC — but does it work in patients, and for which goal?

## 4. Clinical evidence: stronger for maintenance than induction
*~220 words · ¶1 induction · ¶2 maintenance and verdict*

*Grade every study by goal (induce or maintain) and endpoint: symptoms < faecal calprotectin (FC)/C-reactive protein (CRP) < endoscopic healing.*

- **Induction, CD — DINE-CD** (194 adults): symptomatic remission at week 6 was 43.5% on MD vs 46.5% on the specific carbohydrate diet (SCD); FC response 4/13, CRP 1/28 → too few inflamed patients to test an anti-inflammatory effect; meals supplied in weeks 1–6; no normal-diet control. [Lewis 2021]
- **Induction, newer data:** the Crohn's disease exclusion diet (CDED) beat MD, 70.8% vs 38.1% clinical remission at week 12 (small open-label randomised controlled trial, RCT) [Pasta 2025]; meta-analysis: no advantage over control diets (OR 0.98), no endoscopic or histological outcomes. [Singh 2026]
- **Maintenance, UC** (RCT, n = 28, 12 weeks): FC flat on MD (p = 0.375) but rising on the usual diet (p = 0.049); FC >100 µg/g at week 12 in 20% vs 75%; symptoms similar; dietitian-coached; no endoscopy → MD *held* inflammation low. [Haskey 2023]
- **Guidelines:** AGA — MD for all without contraindication "for their overall health and general well-being"; no diet has consistently reduced flares in adults; expert opinion, not graded [Hashash 2024]. **Verify** the ECCO 2025 wording. [Svolos 2025†]
- **An alternative to what?** Not to drugs (no induction advantage, no healing data); only partly to restrictive diets (≈ SCD, < CDED); best as maintenance alongside drugs, especially in UC.
- **Figure 2 (optional, own drawing):** evidence map — rows: induction CD, maintenance UC, meta-analysis; columns: symptoms, FC/CRP, endoscopy; cells ✓ / ± / ✗ / ? with n and duration.

> **Hand-off →** The benefit lies in maintenance, yet both key trials supplied meals or coaching. Can patients sustain MD alone?

## 5. Challenges: evidence, gut tolerance, adherence and access
*~200 words · ¶1 evidence and tolerance · ¶2 adherence, access, weighing*

- **Evidence (most fundamental):** small (28–194), short (mostly 6–12 weeks), unblinded trials for a lifelong disease; varied MD definitions and endpoints; concurrent drugs; no endoscopy [Singh 2026]; observational data look better than RCTs → confounding, reverse causation (sicker patients avoid fibre). [Nada 2026†; Ratajczak 2023]
- **Gut tolerance (keep the biology):** CD strictures (transmural fibrosis) → raw, fibrous plants poorly tolerated → soften by cooking or processing [Dolinger 2024; Hashash 2024]; in active IBD lacking fermenting microbes, intact β-fructan triggers NLRP3/TLR2-dependent cytokines [Armstrong 2023] → fibre helps only if microbes can ferment it.
- **Adherence is part of the mechanism:** EEN-induced microbial and metabolite changes reverted on return to the usual diet [Gerasimidis 2014] → benefit likely lasts only while MD is eaten; real-world adherence is low as patients avoid symptom-provoking foods [Ratajczak 2023]; trial adherence leaned on meals or coaching. [Lewis 2021; Haskey 2023]
- **Access and culture** [v3]: dietitians not routine (AGA only *suggests* access at diagnosis) [Hashash 2024]; cost; adapting MD to non-Mediterranean diets, e.g. a Chinese pilot RCT of a modified MD after CD surgery [Huang 2026†]; apps untested against inflammation. [v3]
- **Weigh them (this is the "evaluate"):** evidence gaps and adherence decide *whether* MD works; tolerance and access are fixable with dietetic support → priorities: longer trials with endoscopic endpoints and supported adherence; identify the active components. [v3]

> **Hand-off →** Weigh the promise against these limits.

## 6. Conclusion
*~70 words · 3–4 sentences · nothing new*

- MD plausibly restores the microbial, barrier and immune arms of a diet-fed loop, most convincingly in colonic disease.
- Trials support holding inflammation low in UC and relieving symptoms in CD (similar to SCD) — not objective remission or mucosal healing.
- Position: a realistic alternative *maintenance* strategy alongside drugs, not instead of them; its value hinges on supported adherence and longer trials with objective endpoints.
