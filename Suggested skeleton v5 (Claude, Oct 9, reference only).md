## 1. Introduction

**¶1 · context · ~80 words.** *Topic claim:* IBD is a lifelong, relapsing disease whose burden is rising fastest where diets are Westernising. [v3]
- **Two diseases:** CD is patchy and transmural, mostly in the terminal ileum and proximal colon (strictures, fistulae); UC is continuous and mucosal, from the rectum into the colon. [Dolinger 2024; Le Berre 2023]
- **Symptoms and consequences:** diarrhoea (bloody in UC), abdominal pain, fatigue, weight loss; bowel damage; colectomy in 10–20% of UC; colorectal cancer risk; poorer quality of life. [Le Berre 2023]
- **Why diet** [v3]: the acceleration in newly industrialised countries implicates environmental, partly dietary, drivers. [Kaplan & Windsor 2021; Godny & Dotan 2024]

**¶2 · question · ~60 words.** *Topic claim:* whether MD counts as an alternative depends on what it would replace.
- **Define:** *management* means inducing, then maintaining, remission for life; *alternative* can mean replacing drugs, replacing restrictive diets, or adding a long-term strategy.
- **Thesis and roadmap:** the evidence supports the third meaning, tested through pathogenesis and drug limits (§2), mechanism (§3), clinical evidence (§4) and challenges (§5).

> **Link (cause):** if drugs already control inflammation, the case for diet must rest on what drugs leave unaddressed.

## 2. Diet-driven inflammation and the limits of drug therapy

**¶1 · mechanism · ~110 words.** *Topic claim:* gut homeostasis is a diet-fed loop linking microbes, the epithelial barrier and immune cells, and a Western diet breaks it. [v3]
- **Healthy loop (Figure 1):** microbes ferment fibre into short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), mainly butyrate; colonocytes oxidise butyrate and consume oxygen, and the resulting hypoxia stabilises hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF), which supports the barrier; butyrate also inhibits histone deacetylases, promoting *Foxp3*⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs). [Parada Venegas 2019]
- **Western-diet break** [v3, corrected]: low fibre lowers butyrate, so oxygen reaches the lumen, dysbiosis follows, and barrier and Treg control weaken [Parada Venegas 2019]; in mice whose epithelium lacks XBP1 or GPX4, excess n-6 or n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) causes endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and chemokine-driven enteritis [Schwärzler 2022]; tumour necrosis factor (TNF)-driven inflammation then sustains the loop.

**¶2 · gap · ~90 words.** *Topic claim:* current drugs act downstream on immune effectors and leave the upstream dietary and microbial drivers unchanged. [v3]
- **Drugs by goal** [v3, broadened]: corticosteroids induce but cannot maintain remission; 5-aminosalicylates work only in mild-to-moderate UC; thiopurines maintain; advanced therapies (anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23 or anti-IL-23 antibodies, JAK inhibitors, S1P modulators) do both; surgery treats refractory disease. [Le Berre 2023; Dolinger 2024]
- **The gap, in numbers:** in anti-TNF-naive CD, 23.8% had primary non-response and 63.1% were not in remission at week 54. [Kennedy 2019]
- **Figure 1 (own drawing, e.g. BioRender):** the loop in health versus on a Western diet, with drug targets at the immune node (downstream) and MD inputs at the microbial and barrier nodes (upstream); cite it in §2 and §3.

> **Link (contrast):** drugs act downstream; the open question is whether a whole diet can restore the upstream inputs.

## 3. How Mediterranean diet components may restore gut homeostasis

**¶1 · mechanism · ~110 words.** *Topic claim:* MD supplies the inputs the loop needs, so it may act upstream of drug targets. [v3]
- **A pattern, not a formula** [v3]: vegetables, legumes, fruit, whole grains, nuts, olive oil and fish, with few ultra-processed foods; the components act together. [Hashash 2024; Godny & Dotan 2024]
- **Fibre to SCFA (Figure 1):** may restore the HIF–barrier and Treg arms; in quiescent UC, faecal SCFA and butyrate were higher after 12 weeks of MD than on the habitual diet. [Haskey 2023; Parada Venegas 2019]
- **Fats and polyphenols** [v3, refined]: olive-oil monounsaturated fat replaces linoleic acid-rich oils, and in susceptible mice soybean oil worsened colitis whereas olive oil did not [Deol 2023†]; polyphenols act partly through microbial metabolites, mostly in preclinical studies. [Godny & Dotan 2024]

**¶2 · limitation · ~60 words.** *Topic claim:* the mechanistic case is stronger for UC than for CD.
- **EEN paradox:** fibre-free exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) induces remission in children with CD even though it lowers butyrate and *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii* (n = 15). [Gerasimidis 2014]
- **Interpretation, not proof:** SCFAs act mainly in the colon, so fibre-based effects may fit colonic, mucosal UC better than often ileal, transmural CD; fish n-3 can also feed the §2 ER-stress route, so its net effect probably depends on dose and genotype. [Schwärzler 2022]

> **Link (consequence):** if this biology holds, benefit should appear mainly in maintenance and in colonic disease; §4 tests that prediction.

## 4. Clinical evidence: stronger for maintenance than induction

*Grading rule:* record each study's goal (induction or maintenance) and endpoint, from weakest to strongest: clinical remission (symptom index) < biomarker response (faecal calprotectin, FC; C-reactive protein, CRP) < mucosal healing (endoscopy).

**¶1 · result (induction) · ~90 words.** *Topic claim:* in active CD, MD matches another diet for symptoms, but trials have not shown that it reduces inflammation.
- **DINE-CD** (194 adults, mild-to-moderate CD): clinical remission at week 6 was 43.5% on MD versus 46.5% on SCD; biomarker response occurred in only 4/13 (FC) and 1/28 (CRP), too few inflamed patients to test an anti-inflammatory effect; meals were supplied in weeks 1–6, and there was no habitual-diet control. [Lewis 2021]
- **Stronger comparators and pooled data:** in a small open-label randomised controlled trial (RCT), CDED outperformed MD (clinical remission 70.8% versus 38.1% at week 12) [Pasta 2025]; a 2026 meta-analysis found no remission advantage over control diets (OR 0.98, 95% CI 0.74–1.30), and none of its eight studies reported mucosal healing. [Singh 2026]

**¶2 · result (maintenance) · ~80 words.** *Topic claim:* the clearest signal is in maintenance of UC, where a small RCT suggests that MD keeps inflammation low.
- **Trial** (n = 28, 12 weeks, quiescent UC): FC was unchanged on MD (p = 0.375) but rose on the habitual diet (p = 0.049), and at week 12 exceeded 100 µg/g in 20% versus 75%; clinical scores were similar; the MD group had dietitian coaching; there was no endoscopy. This is a holding effect rather than a reduction, which is what maintenance requires, and the higher faecal butyrate fits the §3 mechanism. [Haskey 2023]

**¶3 · implication · ~50 words.** *Topic claim:* MD is therefore not an alternative to drugs, and only partly an alternative to restrictive diets.
- **Reasons, with guideline support:** there is no induction advantage and there are no mucosal-healing data. MD matches SCD for symptoms but trails CDED. Its best-supported role is maintenance alongside drugs, especially in UC. The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) advises MD "for their overall health and general well-being" and states that no diet has consistently reduced flares in adults (expert opinion, not graded evidence) [Hashash 2024]; **verify** the ECCO 2025 consensus wording. [Svolos 2025†]
- **Figure 2 (optional, own drawing):** an evidence map with rows for induction in CD, maintenance in UC and the meta-analysis, and columns for clinical remission, FC/CRP and mucosal healing; mark cells ✓ / ± / ✗ / ? and note n and duration.

> **Link (restriction):** the benefit lies in maintenance, which requires years of adherence, yet both key trials supplied meals or coaching.

## 5. Challenges: evidence, gut tolerance, adherence and access

*Write each challenge as a boundary on a claim:* claim affected → constraint → consequence → what remains supported.

**¶1 · limitation (evidence and biology) · ~100 words.** *Topic claim:* two boundaries narrow the maintenance claim. The evidence covers only short-term biomarker control in small UC trials, and gut biology limits who can follow the diet.
- **Evidence** (limits the maintenance claim): small (28–194 patients), mostly short (6–12 weeks), unblinded trials; varied MD definitions and endpoints; concurrent drugs; no mucosal-healing outcomes in the meta-analysed studies [Singh 2026]; observational studies look more favourable than RCTs, which suggests confounding and reverse causation, because sicker patients avoid fibre [Nada 2026†; Ratajczak 2023]. What remains supported: 12-week FC maintenance in quiescent UC.
- **Gut tolerance** (limits applicability): in stricturing CD, transmural fibrosis narrows the lumen, so raw, fibrous plant foods need softening by cooking or processing [Dolinger 2024; Hashash 2024]; in active IBD with depleted fibre-fermenting microbes, unfermented β-fructan fibre triggers NLRP3- and TLR2-dependent cytokine release [Armstrong 2023]. Fibre may therefore help only where microbes can ferment it.

**¶2 · limitation (real-world use) · ~100 words.** *Topic claim:* sustained adherence is the main practical barrier, and the biology makes adherence part of the mechanism.
- **Adherence** (limits transfer from trials to clinics): EEN-induced microbial and metabolite changes reverted when children returned to their habitual diet [Gerasimidis 2014], so a microbe-mediated MD benefit may last only while the diet is eaten; real-world adherence is low, partly because patients avoid symptom-provoking MD foods [Ratajczak 2023], whereas trial adherence relied on supplied meals or coaching. [Lewis 2021; Haskey 2023]
- **Access and culture** [v3] (limits reach, not biology): dietitian access is not routine, since the AGA only suggests it at diagnosis [Hashash 2024]; cost; adaptation to non-Mediterranean food cultures, as in a Chinese pilot RCT of a modified MD after CD surgery [Huang 2026†]; app-based support untested against inflammation. [v3]
- **Weighing and next test:** evidence gaps and adherence decide whether MD works at all, whereas tolerance and access respond to dietetic support; the most informative next trial would last at least a year, use mucosal-healing endpoints and compare supported with unsupported adherence, with identifying the active components to follow. [v3]

> **Link (consequence):** these boundaries set how far the answer to the title can reach.

## 6. Conclusion

*About 70 words in 3–4 sentences, nothing new; one sentence per slot:*

1. **Contribution:** MD is best understood as an alternative long-term maintenance strategy used alongside drugs, not a replacement for them.
2. **Decisive evidence:** small trials suggest it keeps inflammation low in quiescent UC and matches another diet for symptoms in CD, but no trial to date shows that it induces biomarker response or mucosal healing.
3. **Implication:** because its biology favours colonic disease and requires continuous intake, dietetic support belongs to the treatment itself.
4. **Boundary:** its future role depends on longer trials with mucosal-healing endpoints and supported adherence.
