## 1. Introduction

**¶1 · context · ~70 words.** *Topic claim:* IBD is a lifelong, relapsing disease whose burden is rising fastest where diets are Westernising. [v3]
- **Two diseases:** CD is patchy and transmural, mostly in the terminal ileum and proximal colon, and causes strictures and fistulae. UC is continuous and mucosal, running from the rectum into the colon. [Dolinger 2024; Le Berre 2023]
- **Symptoms and consequences:** diarrhoea (bloody in UC), abdominal pain, fatigue and weight loss. Longer term: bowel damage, colectomy in 10–20% of people with UC, colorectal cancer risk and poorer quality of life. [Le Berre 2023]
- **Why diet** [v3]: incidence is rising in newly industrialised regions that are adopting Western diets. This implicates diet as an environmental driver. [Godny & Dotan 2024]

**¶2 · question · ~40 words.** *Topic claim:* whether MD counts as an alternative depends on which part of management it serves.
- **Define and claim:** *management* means inducing remission, then maintaining it, for life. Thesis: drugs can break the disrupted loop, and MD may keep the healthy loop running. That makes MD a maintenance alternative alongside drugs, not a replacement.
- **Roadmap:** §2 compares the healthy and disrupted loops. §3 maps MD onto each node. §4 tests this in patients. §5 sets the limits.

> **Link (cause):** the case for diet rests on how diet shapes the gut loop, in health and in IBD.

## 2. How the healthy gut loop breaks in IBD, and where drugs act

**¶1 · mechanism (healthy loop) · ~70 words.** *Topic claim:* in health, diet feeds a self-reinforcing loop that keeps the gut quiet. [v3]
- **Healthy loop (Figure 1A):** [Litvak 2018; Parada Venegas 2019; Chassaing 2015]
  - ① Microbes ferment fibre into short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), mainly butyrate.
  - ② Butyrate activates the epithelial receptor PPARγ, which drives mitochondrial β-oxidation in colonocytes. The high oxygen use keeps the epithelial surface hypoxic (below 1% oxygen). Hypoxia stabilises hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF), which protects the barrier, and mucus keeps bacteria at a distance.
  - ③ Butyrate inhibits histone deacetylases and signals through GPR43 and GPR109A. This promotes *Foxp3*⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs) and IL-10, and restrains NF-κB.
  - The hypoxic surface keeps the lumen anaerobic, so the obligate anaerobes that make butyrate stay dominant, which closes the loop.

**¶2 · mechanism (disrupted loop) · ~140 words.** *Topic claim:* a Western diet weakens each node, and inflammation can then lock the loop into a self-sustaining disrupted state. [v3, expanded]
- **Nodes ① and ② break** [v3, corrected]:
  - Low fibre means fewer butyrate producers and less butyrate. [Parada Venegas 2019]
  - Without butyrate, PPARγ falls silent and colonocytes switch to glycolysis. They consume less oxygen, so epithelial hypoxia is lost.
  - The escaping oxygen, plus nitrate from inflammatory iNOS, lets facultative anaerobes such as *Escherichia coli* expand. [Litvak 2018]
  - In UC, colonocytes show lower PPARγ and oxidise less butyrate, and the mucus layer is thinner. [Litvak 2018]
  - In mice:
    - soybean oil rich in linoleic acid favoured adherent-invasive *E. coli* and a leaky barrier [Deol 2023];
    - dietary emulsifiers let bacteria encroach through the mucus towards the epithelium. [Chassaing 2015]
  - Where XBP1 or GPX4 defences fail, excess n-6 or n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) causes endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress in the epithelium. [Schwärzler 2022]
- **Node ③ breaks and the loop closes (Figure 1B):** [Litvak 2018; Parada Venegas 2019]
  - Fewer Tregs and unrestrained NF-κB raise inflammatory signalling, including tumour necrosis factor (TNF).
  - These signals push colonocytes further towards glycolysis, so dysbiosis and inflammation feed each other. The loop may then sustain itself even after the dietary trigger is removed.

*Budget note: the core of this paragraph is the human-relevant chain: butyrate → PPARγ → oxygen → E. coli → inflammation. If §2 runs over 280 words, move the three mouse findings (soybean oil, emulsifiers, PUFA) into Figure 1B.*

**¶3 · gap · ~70 words.** *Topic claim:* current drugs act mainly on node ③ and can break the disrupted loop, but no drug restores node ①. [v3]
- **Drugs by goal** [v3, broadened]: [Le Berre 2023; Dolinger 2024]
  - Corticosteroids induce remission but cannot maintain it.
  - Thiopurines maintain remission.
  - Advanced therapies do both: anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23 or anti-IL-23 antibodies, JAK inhibitors and S1P modulators.
  - 5-aminosalicylates, used in mild-to-moderate UC, are the partial exception to downstream action: they act on colonocytes as PPARγ agonists, at node ②. [Litvak 2018]
  - Surgery is still needed for refractory disease, including colectomy in 10–20% of people with UC.
- **Figure 1 (own drawing, e.g. BioRender), three panels:**
  - (A) the healthy loop;
  - (B) the disrupted loop on a Western diet, with drug targets at node ③;
  - (C) the restored loop, with MD inputs at nodes ① and ② (used in §3).
  - Keep the node numbers identical across panels.

> **Link (consequence):** drugs can break the disrupted loop, but none supplies its fermentation input. The question is whether a diet can restore the upstream nodes.

## 3. How Mediterranean diet components may restore the loop

**¶1 · mechanism (nodes ① and ②) · ~120 words.** *Topic claim:* MD supplies inputs for both upstream nodes, including fermentation, which no drug supplies. [v3]
- **Node ① fermentation** [v3]: fibre, legumes and whole grains feed the butyrate producers. In quiescent UC, after 12 weeks on MD, faecal SCFA and butyrate were higher than on the habitual diet, with more of the SCFA producer *Ruminococcus bromii*. [Haskey 2023; Parada Venegas 2019]
- **Node ② epithelium** [v3, refined]:
  - Butyrate reactivates epithelial PPARγ, the same receptor that 5-aminosalicylates target. This should restore β-oxidation, oxygen use, hypoxia and HIF-driven barrier protection. PPARγ is also thought to upregulate tight-junction proteins. [Litvak 2018; Parada Venegas 2019]
  - Fewer ultra-processed foods mean less exposure to emulsifiers. [Chassaing 2015]
  - Olive oil's monounsaturated fat replaces oils rich in linoleic acid, and olive oil did not worsen colitis in susceptible mice. [Deol 2023]
  - Most of the evidence for this node comes from preclinical studies.

**¶2 · mechanism and boundary (node ③) · ~100 words.** *Topic claim:* restoring nodes ① and ② may re-tune node ③, but only where the loop is still partly intact.
- **Node ③ immune restraint** [v3]:
  - As SCFA supply recovers, butyrate-driven Tregs, IL-10 and NF-κB restraint should return. [Parada Venegas 2019]
  - Polyphenols add anti-inflammatory microbial metabolites. [Godny & Dotan 2024]
  - In the UC trial, faecal secretory IgA rose on MD. [Haskey 2023]
  - Caveat: n-3 fats from fish also feed the ER-stress route, so their effect probably depends on dose and genotype. [Schwärzler 2022]
- **Boundary:**
  - In UC, colonocytes have less PPARγ and oxidise less butyrate, so extra substrate may not be used while inflammation persists. [Litvak 2018]
  - Where fermenting microbes are lost, intact fibre can itself trigger inflammation. [Armstrong 2023]
  - MD is therefore better placed to hold a loop that drugs have calmed than to repair one in full disruption.
  - SCFAs act mainly in the colon, so the case for MD may be stronger in UC than in ileal CD.

> **Link (consequence):** this predicts a maintenance benefit, visible as stable inflammation and recovering node readouts. §4 tests that prediction.

## 4. Human evidence: holding remission, not inducing it

**¶1 · result (maintenance) · ~110 words.** *Topic claim:* in quiescent UC, MD held inflammation low and moved the node readouts in the predicted direction.
- **Trial** (randomised controlled trial (RCT), n = 28, 12 weeks): [Haskey 2023]
  - Faecal calprotectin (FC) directly measures intestinal inflammation, and levels above 100 µg/g predict relapse.
  - FC stayed unchanged on MD (p = 0.375) but rose on the habitual diet (p = 0.049). At week 12 it exceeded 100 µg/g in 20% of the MD group versus 75% of the habitual-diet group.
  - The node ① readouts (faecal SCFA and butyrate) were higher, and the node ③ readout (secretory IgA) rose.
  - Symptom scores were similar. The MD group had dietitian coaching, and there was no endoscopy.

**¶2 · comparison (induction) · ~60 words.** *Topic claim:* the same diet has not been shown to break an active disrupted loop, so the evidence does not support replacing drugs.
- **Induction:**
  - In active CD, MD matched the specific carbohydrate diet (SCD) for clinical remission (43.5% versus 46.5%). [Lewis 2021]
  - Biomarker response was rare, however: 4 of 13 for FC and 1 of 28 for C-reactive protein (CRP). [Lewis 2021]
  - A 2026 meta-analysis found no remission advantage over control diets (OR 0.98) and no mucosal-healing data. [Singh 2026]
- **Guidelines and verdict:**
  - The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) advises MD "for their overall health and general well-being". It also states that no diet has consistently reduced flares in adults. This is expert opinion. [Hashash 2024]
  - **Verify** the wording of the 2025 ECCO consensus. [Svolos 2025†]
  - MD is therefore a maintenance alternative alongside drugs, not a replacement.
- **Figure 2 (optional, own drawing):** a node-by-node evidence map. Rows: ①, ② and ③. Columns: MD input, human readout, and evidence level (human RCT, mouse, or inferred).

> **Link (restriction):** the benefit depends on a partly intact loop and on continuous intake, and both conditions set the challenges.

## 5. Challenges: weak evidence, fibre intolerance and lapsed adherence

*Write each challenge as a boundary on a claim. For each, give the claim affected, the constraint, the consequence, and what remains supported.*

**¶1 · limitation (evidence and biology) · ~80 words.** *Topic claim:* two limits narrow the restoration claim: thin evidence, and loops too disrupted to accept the restoring inputs.
- **Evidence:**
  - The trials are small (28–194 patients), short (6–12 weeks) and unblinded. They define MD differently, patients stay on their drugs, and none reports mucosal healing. [Singh 2026]
  - Human data cover the node ① and ③ readouts only; restoration of node ② is inferred from mice.
  - What remains supported: 12 weeks of FC maintenance in quiescent UC.
- **Too disrupted to restore:**
  - In stricturing CD, transmural fibrosis narrows the lumen, so food needs to be softer and less fibrous. [Dolinger 2024; Hashash 2024]
  - Active IBD with depleted fermenting microbes reacts to intact β-fructan fibre through NLRP3 and TLR2. [Armstrong 2023]
  - So the patients with the most disrupted loops tolerate the restoring inputs least.

**¶2 · limitation (real-world use) · ~80 words.** *Topic claim:* restoration lasts only while the inputs continue, so adherence is part of the mechanism.
- **Continuous inputs:**
  - Microbial and metabolite changes caused by exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) reverted when children returned to their habitual diet. So a loop restored by MD may drift back when the diet stops. [Gerasimidis 2014]
  - Real-world adherence is low, because patients avoid foods that provoke symptoms. [Ratajczak 2023]
  - Trial adherence, by contrast, relied on supplied meals or coaching. [Lewis 2021; Haskey 2023]
- **Access and culture** [v3]:
  - Dietitian access is not routine; the AGA only suggests it at diagnosis. [Hashash 2024]
  - The diet costs money.
  - MD must be adapted to non-Mediterranean food cultures, as in a Chinese pilot RCT of a modified MD after CD surgery. [Huang 2026†]
  - App-based support is untested against inflammation. [v3]
- **Weighing and next test:**
  - Evidence gaps and adherence decide whether MD works at all. Tolerance and access respond to dietetic support.
  - The decisive trial would last at least a year and use mucosal-healing endpoints. It would track the node readouts (faecal SCFA, secretory IgA) and compare supported with unsupported adherence.
  - Identifying the active components would follow. [v3]

> **Link (consequence):** weigh the restoration case against these limits to answer the title.

## 6. Conclusion

*About 60 words in 3–4 sentences, with nothing new. Write one sentence per slot:*

1. **Contribution:** IBD can be read as a healthy gut loop turned into a self-sustaining disrupted loop. MD supplies inputs that may restore its fermentation, epithelial and immune nodes.
2. **Decisive evidence:** a small trial suggests that MD holds inflammation low in quiescent UC while the node readouts recover. No trial shows that it induces remission or mucosal healing.
3. **Implication:** MD therefore fits after drugs have broken the disrupted loop, as a maintenance strategy that keeps the healthy loop running.
4. **Boundary:** its benefit lasts only while the diet is eaten, and it awaits longer trials with mucosal-healing endpoints.
