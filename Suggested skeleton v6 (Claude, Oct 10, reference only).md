*Claude, 10 October 2026. Reference only; write your own plan. Sources: v5's notes.*

**Title:** The Mediterranean diet as an alternative for the management of inflammatory bowel diseases

**Thesis:** drugs break the disrupted gut loop; the Mediterranean diet (MD) may keep the healthy loop running: maintenance alongside drugs, not replacement.

### 1. Introduction (~100)
- **Two diseases:** Crohn's disease (CD), patchy and transmural; ulcerative colitis (UC), continuous and mucosal.
- **Burden:** diarrhoea, bleeding, pain, fatigue; strictures, colectomy, cancer risk.
- **Why diet:** incidence rises where diets Westernise.
- **Question:** alternative for inducing or maintaining remission?

### 2. How the healthy gut loop breaks, and where drugs act (~280)
- **Loop:** ① fermentation makes butyrate; ② butyrate sets epithelial energy and oxygen; ③ immune tone feeds back. Inputs: fibre and fat quality.
- **① Fermentation.** Healthy: anaerobes make butyrate from fibre. Disrupted: fewer producers; leaking oxygen lets *E. coli* expand.
- **② Epithelium.** Healthy: butyrate activates PPARγ; colonocytes burn fatty acids and stay hypoxic. Disrupted: glycolysis, lost hypoxia; seed-oil linoleic acid raises membrane arachidonic acid.
- **③ Immune mediators.** Healthy: regulatory T cells, n-3 pro-resolving mediators. Disrupted: TNF, arachidonic-acid prostaglandins. Inflammation then sustains the loop; low butyrate may be a consequence, not only a cause.
- **Current treatment:** 5-aminosalicylates (mild UC), steroids (induction), thiopurines (maintenance), advanced therapies (both), surgery last. Drugs act mainly at ③; none corrects either input.

### 3. How MD components may restore the loop (~230)
- **Fibre answers ① and ②:** producers return; butyrate should re-engage PPARγ, which 5-aminosalicylates also target.
- **Olive oil answers ② by substitution:** replacing seed oils cuts the n-6 supply for arachidonic acid.
- **Fish answers ③:** EPA and DHA yield pro-resolving mediators; fish-oil capsules alone did not maintain CD remission, so the pattern matters.
- **Boundary:** inflamed colonocytes use less butyrate; fibre can inflame guts lacking fermenters; susceptible epithelium reacts to excess polyunsaturated fat. So MD holds calmed loops, not active ones.

### 4. Human evidence: holding remission, not inducing it (~170)
- **Maintenance:** in quiescent UC, calprotectin stayed flat on MD, rose on habitual diet; faecal butyrate and secretory IgA rose.
- **Asymmetry:** only fibre has a measured MD response; fat has stronger disease-risk data, no intervention readout.
- **Induction:** in active CD, clinical remission matched the specific carbohydrate diet but biomarkers rarely improved; a 2026 meta-analysis found no advantage. US guidance endorses MD for general health, not flare prevention.

### 5. Challenges: thin evidence, intolerance, lapsed adherence (~160)
- **Evidence:** small, short, unblinded trials; no mucosal healing; fat arm unmeasured.
- **Unrepairable loops:** strictures, lost fermenters, susceptible epithelium. The most disrupted loops tolerate repair least.
- **Adherence is mechanism:** diet-driven microbial changes revert when diets stop; real-world adherence is low.
- **Access:** dietitians, cost, non-Mediterranean cuisines.
- **Next test:** year-long trials, mucosal-healing endpoints, readouts for both axes.

### 6. Conclusion (~60)
- MD reverses both inputs and may restore all three nodes.
- It holds remission in UC but has not induced it, so it belongs after drugs.
- Benefit lasts only while eaten; longer trials must settle its role.
