## B. Scientific accuracy

7. **PUFA and dysbiosis (checked on PubMed).**
   - Supported: **n-6 linoleic acid** causes dysbiosis in susceptible mice. A high-linoleic-acid soybean-oil diet expanded adherent-invasive *E. coli*, increased barrier permeability and worsened colitis; a low-linoleic-acid oil and olive oil did not (Deol et al., 2023, https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2023.2229945). Corn oil also favoured pathobionts (Abulizi et al., 2019, https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11020418).
   - Not shown in these abstracts: that this dysbiosis *lowers SCFA*. Keep that step out unless a full text states it. **(Full text needed if you want to keep it.)**
   - Contradicted: **n-3** causing dysbiosis. Omega-3 tends to *increase* butyrate-producing bacteria (Costantini et al., 2017, review, https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms18122645). Remove n-3 from the dysbiosis claim. This also resolves the clash with recommending oily fish.
   - The ER-stress route (Schwärzler et al., 2022) involves both n-3 and n-6 PUFAs and was shown in hosts with impaired GPX4 or XBP1. Keep "in susceptible hosts" in the wording.
8. **Corticosteroids do not "target TNF alpha".** They are broadly immunosuppressive. Reword the therapy bullet so that only anti-TNF agents target TNF.
9. **"Indirectly activate T-cells"** is still attached to PUFAs. Replace it with the specific, supported effects (dysbiosis, barrier leak, epithelial ER stress leading to neutrophil recruitment).

## C. Depth that the rubric rewards (Content 30%)

10. **The MD mechanism is still generic.** The rubric's 60–69 band is described as "Some aspects of the essay may not make good reference to the biology of the proposed alternative treat[ment]". Name the steps: fibre fermented to butyrate supports epithelial energy use and barrier function (HIF), and promotes colonic regulatory T cells (Foxp3); olive-oil MUFA replaces n-6-rich oils; polyphenols are converted by microbes into anti-inflammatory metabolites.
11. **Clinical evidence is not specific.** Plan two questions, as you suggested:
    - *Keeping inflammation low (maintenance):* UC in remission, with calprotectin as the marker.
    - *Reducing active disease (induction):* DINE-CD (adults, mild-to-moderate CD). MD and the specific carbohydrate diet gave similar symptomatic remission at week 6 (43.5% vs 46.5%), but calprotectin and CRP responses were uncommon (https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2021.05.047).
12. **EEN comparison (I revised my view after your point).** It is valid on the induction question. State the gap: EEN brings mucosal healing (mainly shown in paediatric CD), while MD's induction effect in DINE-CD was mostly symptomatic.
